Liner Notes — a quieter, richer Music Assistant

A redesign that borrows Roon’s magazine-like seriousness and pairs it with the art-driven immersion of Plexamp and Apple Music, then fixes what Music Assistant users complain about most: not knowing which speaker they’re controlling, imprecise volume, and big libraries that are hard to get around. Everything below is clickable, in both themes.

01The art is the only colourChrome stays neutral grey, like Roon. Colour comes from the record that’s playing: washes, Now Playing, room cards.
02Editorial typeA display serif names things (artists, albums, rooms, big numbers). Inter does everything functional.
03Rooms are first-classThe room you’re controlling is always named and one click away. Groups get a real mixer.
04Never crop the coverAlbum art is shown whole and square, with captions beneath it — no text stamped on top.

02

What the research says

I read Roon’s theme file and help centre, sampled colours from real screenshots, and surveyed Plexamp, Apple Music, Spotify, Tidal, Qobuz, the Sonos redesign, and indie players. I also read about 60 Music Assistant discussions to see what users actually ask for. Below is what each one taught, and what this design takes from it.

Roon

The reference · 1.8 design, refined through 2.74
  • A “museum” and a “music magazine”. Neutral space, bold editorial type: Grifo for titles, Lato for everything else.
  • Neutral chrome, one accent. Page #181818, panels #242424, accent #6B6ED9. It never tints the UI from artwork, but it blurs the art behind Now Playing.
  • Footer has the room picker. The zone button sits bottom-right next to volume, with a signal-path light and a waveform seek bar.
  • Focus, linked credits and reviews are what fans love most.
  • Complaints: the greeting wastes space, credits shown as photo bubbles, grey-on-grey contrast, lyrics taking over Now Playing, nothing can be reordered.
#181818#242424#6B6ED9#525F9A band#A8A8A8
TakenSerif + sans split, neutral greys, violet accent, footer room button, waveform seek, signal-path dot, Focus, Play-now split button, stat tiles, linked credits. Credits become a list grouped by role, and the greeting is kept compact.

Plexamp

The self-hosted favourite
  • “Ultrablur”. A gradient of several colours from four corner colours of the art. Plex computes them on the server and caches them.
  • Background styles: Flat / Smooth / Ultrablur. Colours can be frozen, or you can pick plain Black / White themes.
  • The seek bar and buttons are tinted with the album’s colours.
  • Complaint: text becomes unreadable on some “Smooth” backgrounds, because there’s no contrast check.
TakenNow Playing’s drifting ultrablur. Music Assistant already gets an artwork palette from its server (paletteFromServer), so this is cheap. Text sits on a veil so it stays readable.

Apple Music · Spotify

Mainstream patterns
  • Apple: line-by-line lyrics with the current line bright and the rest dimmed; animated art; the AirPlay picker with a checkbox and slider for each speaker. Its Tahoe glass is criticised as “much harder to read”.
  • Spotify: a Now Playing panel on the right with stacked cards. Connect names the device in colour: “Playing on Kitchen”.
  • Spotify forcing its panel open, and dropping full-screen lyrics, both drew backlash.
TakenThe lyrics typography, click a line to seek, the room always named in the player, and a panel that opens only when you ask for it.

Tidal · Qobuz

Hi-res and liner notes
  • Tidal: credits are one tap away and link to each contributor’s other work. Removing them in 2025 caused an outcry.
  • Qobuz: reviews, bios, digital booklets and the file’s technical details shown alongside the art. The background adapts to the cover.
  • Tidal’s “Max” label hides the real sample rate, and some devices quietly play at a lower quality.
TakenCredits on the album page, an “About this album” rail, and quality labels that show the real format each speaker receives.

Sonos

The cautionary tale
  • The 2024 rewrite shipped without queue editing, a sleep timer, local library search or a volume number. It cost $20–30M and the CEO stepped down.
  • The recovery: tabs came back, a System view lists every room (sortable by playing / A–Z), group badges show how many rooms are in a group, and Play lets you pick the room.
TakenThe Rooms drawer sorted by Playing / A–Z, “Play in…” under the Play button, a group count on the room button. Also a lesson: change things gradually, never as one big rewrite.

Indie players

Albums · Doppler · Spite · Feishin · Marvis
  • Doppler uses the whole window width for album grids. Spite: it shouldn’t “feel like managing a spreadsheet”.
  • Albums has smart filters you can save, and anniversaries.
  • Feishin has a full-screen player with tabs (Up next / Lyrics / Related) and keeps “Up next” in place when you shuffle.
  • Marvis has deep customisation, but its default look is “bland”.
TakenDense grids that fill the width, Focus saved as a smart playlist, the tabbed Now Playing panel, and good defaults rather than a pile of settings.

03

Every screen, both themes

The same screens rendered dark and light from one set of tokens. In light mode the art washes are mixed with white, Now Playing becomes frosted rather than smoky, and the activity band turns into a saturated violet, as in Roon’s light theme.

04

Mobile

Same tokens and type. Navigation moves to a bottom tab bar with a dedicated Rooms tab, and the mini player always names the room. These follow the theme, accent and typeface controls above.

05

Design system

Colour values follow Roon’s theme file closely (dark #181818 / #242424; light #FFFFFF / #F7F8F9). The accent is a single hue with a dark-mode and a light-mode value. Signal colours are fixed so their meaning never changes with the accent.

Colour tokens

Motion: hovers 150 ms; panels and popovers 220–450 ms on cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1); the artwork tint crossfades over 900 ms using registered CSS properties. The drifting ultrablur and equaliser bars stop when prefers-reduced-motion is set.

06

How to build it in this repo

Upstream is already mid-redesign: 2.11 has new artist and album pages, and is moving from Vuetify to shadcn-vue / Tailwind. So this should land as small, token-first steps that rebase cleanly, not a big rewrite like Sonos’s.

STEP 1 · FOUNDATIONS

Tokens, type, theme

  • Replace the light/dark values in src/styles/style.css (:root and .dark) with the token table, and mirror them in src/plugins/vuetify.ts. Both systems must stay in step.
  • Add the display serif plus Inter, retiring Roboto in vite.config.ts, and a .font-display utility. Update h1–h6 in global.css.
  • Swap the primary colour from #03a9f4 to the accent token; add --sig-* colours.
STEP 2 · SHELL

Sidebar, top bar, player bar

  • AppSidebar.vue: quieter nav, a live Rooms section in place of (or above) Shortcuts.
  • Top bar: back/forward and a search pill that opens the existing CommandCenter.
  • PlayerOSD/Player.vue: serif title, waveform seek as the default, signal dot, − / + volume, room button with a group count.
STEP 3 · ROOMS

Rooms drawer

  • Turn PlayerSelect.vue from a popover into a right-hand drawer: sort by Playing / A–Z; the selected group expands into a member mixer with “+ Room” chips and Transfer queue.
  • Reuse PlayerCard logic for power, volume and child volumes.
STEP 4 · PAGES

Album, artist, library

  • InfoHeader.vue: art-tinted wash from the palette and a compact header, so tracks show without scrolling.
  • AlbumDetails.vue: right rail with About, Credits, Available from (from ProviderDetails), Versions and Tags.
  • ArtistDetails.vue: tabs instead of stacked sections; discography before the bio.
  • ItemsListing.vue: Focus chips, a visible sort chip, letter sections and an A–Z scrubber.
STEP 5 · NOW PLAYING

Immersive view

  • PlayerFullscreen.vue: ultrablur from the palette with a veil and grain, large cover, serif title, a frosted right panel with tabs (Up next / Lyrics / Credits / Artist).
  • Keep the existing visualizer as another background option, with a “freeze colours” setting.
STEP 6 · HOME

Discover → Home

  • HomeWidgetRows.vue rows: a compact greeting with stats, an “Around the house” rooms row, a Recent activity band, new releases, playlists, radio and a genre mosaic.
  • Keep row reordering and hiding (users asked for it; Roon can’t do it).
Prototype data is fictional. Album covers are generated SVGs; artist and genre photos are free-licence images from Unsplash. Display faces are from Google Fonts — Grifo, Roon’s own serif, is commercial, so Playfair Display stands in by default.
